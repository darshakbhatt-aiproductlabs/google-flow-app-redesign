The trust contract — why the user presses one button

The plan-and-confirm screen (4B) and the per-shot regenerate screen (4C) together form the trust contract that makes a "generate the whole thing" button safe to press. Four commitments:

  • Single commit, transparent estimate. The full breakdown — 4 video shots × 80 + 2 image shots × 30 + voiceover 60 + stitch 20 = 460 credits — is visible before anything generates. No mid-flow surprises. Estimate tolerance ±5 %, surplus refunded.
  • Two free regens per shot. ElevenLabs popularised this with voice. Generative AI is non-deterministic; charging full credits on every retry erodes trust. The counter is visible per shot: "2 free regens left" → "1 free left" → honest credit cost.
  • Restitch & export is free. Generation is the expensive part; stitching is cheap. Making it free removes friction on iteration.
  • "Tweak ✎" opens the standard creator panel (chapter 3, frame 3A). Power-users break out into the full playground for any one shot — prompt edit, model swap, seed lock, motion brush — and the result drops back into the storyboard.
  • SynthID + C2PA encode the source notebook ID in the provenance manifest. Verifiable origin — a moat no peer can claim.
  • Per-tier limits keep economics honest — Creator: up to 6 shots / 1 free regen per shot. Pro: up to 12 shots / 2 free regens per shot / Gemini Advanced bundled for ingestion. Studio: unlimited shots / brand kit auto-applied / provenance export.
  • This is glue, not a silo. Notebook flow uses Storyboard mode under the hood, opens shots in the standard creator panel, deposits assets in the Library as a smart collection, and routes the final stitch to YouTube Shorts (chapter 5).

The defensible Google moat almost nobody else can match cheaply. SynthID is Google DeepMind's invisible pixel-watermark; C2PA is the industry standard manifest. Both ship on by default, can't be turned off, travel with every export (including YouTube). Flow as the trust-first generation product is the right brand position for Google — and the right answer to growing deepfake regulation.

Provenance is platform, not feature

SynthID survives crop, recompress, and screenshot. C2PA encodes the model, edits and timestamp cryptographically. Together they answer: was this made with AI? when? by whom? from what source?

Where this also shows up

The Notebook flow (chapter 4) encodes the source notebook ID into the C2PA manifest, so the chain is verifiable from grounded source to final video. Public verification tool ships Phase 5.

Cameos with face verification — no public registry

Flow borrows Sora 2's face-verification step (turn-head clip) so likeness can't be stolen. But unlike Sora, Flow refuses to host "public" cameos at all. Three permission tiers:

  • Only me ✓ (default)
  • Studio teammates — explicit add
  • Public — disabled by policy. Flow does not host a public cameo registry.
Why this matters

Sora's open cameo registry triggered an abuse cycle within weeks of launch. Google can't ship that. The 3-tier model is the safety prerequisite for a serious character system.

Chapter 09

Scalability: six axes Flow is architecting for.

Not all of these ship at launch, but the architecture must support them. The Notebook flow (chapter 4) is the first concrete instance of axis 3 — the rest fall into place behind it.

1 · Multi-model routing

Each shot in a storyboard can route to a different engine — Veo Lite for drafts, Veo Quality for hero shots, Imagen for stills. Pro+ shows per-shot cost preview. Higgsfield's approach minus the third-party aggregation; Flow stays Google-models-first.

2 · Real-time canvas

Krea's sub-50 ms regeneration is the new bar for image work. Pro+ "Live preview" toggle on the image playground (chapter 3, 3B) once GPU pipelines support it. Path: Imagen 4 fast-path → on-device draft using Gemini Nano on Pixel for the lightest pass.

3 · Workflow agents

From a Notebook (chapter 4) is the first shipped agent. Phase 4 adds: marketing agent ("4 ad variants for different age groups, render 9:16, publish to YouTube Shorts with A/B metadata"), sales agent ("turn the last 6 customer-call transcripts into a 60s product reel"), publishing agent ("12-shot weekly newsletter video from my latest blog post"). All reuse the plan / commit / regen pattern.

4 · Region-aware models

Indic LoRAs on Imagen / Veo for Indian faces, fashion, architecture, regional vehicles. Hinglish prompt understanding (chapter 5, 5D). A hard moat — Western model labs won't ship this fast, and India is one of Flow's anchor markets.

5 · Provenance as platform

SynthID + C2PA on every output (chapter 6) is the start. Future: shared C2PA manifest format with YouTube, Photos and Drive so the chain is verifiable across the Google ecosystem. For Notebook flows, the manifest encodes the source notebook ID — a verifiable origin claim. Public verification tool ships Phase 5.

6 · Trust as differentiator

Three things make Flow's promise different from competitors': (a) provenance default-on, (b) single credit-commit with free regens (chapter 4), (c) face-verification for cameos with no public registry (chapter 6). Together they form a brand position no peer can credibly take — and one Google specifically needs to occupy.
